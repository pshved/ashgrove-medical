Who we treat

  • Rashes or skin breakdown beneath the breast fold

Common questions

Before you call

Anything else, our patient coordinators answer the phone in person from 8:00 to 17:30.

Will insurance cover my reduction?

Often, yes. Most plans cover reduction when symptoms are documented and the planned tissue removal meets the insurer's minimum. Our pre-authorisation estimator gives a first indication.

Can I still breastfeed afterwards?

Many patients can. Pedicle techniques keep the nipple attached to its underlying tissue, which preserves ducts in most cases. Your surgeon will explain the odds for the technique planned for you.

How visible are the scars?

Scars depend on technique. They are pink for several months and usually fade to fine pale lines by the end of the first year.

How long until I can go back to work?

Most desk-based patients return after 10 to 14 days. Physically demanding jobs need four to six weeks.

Can I keep what you remove?

No. We are asked this about once a week, and the answer has not changed since 2012.

Recovery

What the first year looks like

Recovery is gradual and predictable. This is the schedule most Ashgrove patients follow after a Wise-pattern or vertical reduction.

  1. Day 0

    Home the same evening

    You wake in a soft surgical bra with dressings. A responsible adult drives you home and stays the first night.

  2. Days 1–3

    Rest and short walks

    Discomfort is usually controlled with paracetamol and a short course of stronger medication. Walking every few hours reduces the risk of clots.

  3. Day 7

    First review

    Dressings are changed and any drains removed. Most patients notice that their back and neck already feel different.

  4. Weeks 2–3

    Back to desk work

    You can drive once you can turn and brake without hesitation. Showering normally is allowed from day 10.

  5. Weeks 4–6

    Exercise resumes

    Lower-body training and brisk walking first, then running and upper-body work from week six. Support garment day and night until then.

  6. Month 3

    Final shape

    Swelling has settled. This is the right time to be measured for new bras, and to dispose of the old ones with whatever ceremony you feel they deserve.

  7. Month 12

    Scars mature

    Scars soften and fade from pink to pale. Your final review includes standardised photographs compared with your first visit.

When to call us straight away

Fever above 38 °C, one breast suddenly larger or more painful than the other, a darkening nipple, or calf pain or breathlessness. Our on-call surgeon is reachable 24 hours a day on the post-operative line given to you at discharge.

What insurers usually ask for

  • Symptoms documented by your physician for at least six months
  • Six to twelve weeks of conservative treatment, such as physical therapy or a supportive bra fitting
  • Photographs taken at consultation
  • A planned removal that meets the insurer's minimum for your body size
About the minimum

Many insurers set the minimum with the Schnur sliding scale, which links grams removed per breast to your body surface area. Larger frames require more tissue removed to qualify.
